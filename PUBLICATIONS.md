# Published Work and Research Lineage

This page records published scholarship by or coauthored by Jeremiah "Jay" Ratican that is relevant to the Artificial Life Research Journal.

The purpose is not to treat publication as evidence that later software experiments are correct. The purpose is intellectual provenance: to identify which questions, assumptions, and conceptual structures were already present in formal scholarship before they were implemented or tested in later prototypes.

Lindenwood University Digital Commons is preferred as the primary source because it provides institutional publication records and, for many papers, full-text copies. ResearchGate is retained as a secondary profile and discovery index.

## Direct intellectual ancestors of the current research program

### PUB-001: A Proposed Meta-Reality Immersive Development Pipeline

**Ratican, J., Hutson, J., & Wright, A. (2023). A proposed meta-reality immersive development pipeline: Generative AI models and extended reality (XR) content for the metaverse. _Journal of Intelligent Learning Systems and Applications_.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.4236/jilsa.2023.151002

Published: February 7, 2023.

Why it matters here: this paper predates the persona and organism work but establishes several recurring concerns: AI-generated persistent virtual environments, game-engine integration, scalable content generation, interoperability, and the use of AI as a component inside a larger interactive system rather than as the entire system.

Related journal lines: persistent worlds, embodiment, game-engine integration, PRJ-021 Pooka, later generative-world infrastructure.

### PUB-002: The Six Emotional Dimension (6DE) Model

**Ratican, J., & Hutson, J. (2023). The Six Emotional Dimension (6DE) Model: A multidimensional approach to analyzing human emotions and unlocking the potential of emotionally intelligent artificial intelligence via large language models. _DS Journal of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics, 1_(1), 44-52.**

Published: May 5, 2023.

The six dimensions were arousal, valence, dominance, agency, fidelity, and novelty.

Why it matters here: 6DE established explicit affective state as something that could be represented separately from free-form language generation. Later companion and persona systems repeatedly return to persistent affect, modulation, and the question of whether emotional continuity can live outside the renderer.

Related records: PRJ-033 Six Emotional Dimension Model; RQ-001 Persistent Artificial Identity; RQ-006 Artificiality and Believability.

### PUB-003: Life, Death, and AI

**Hutson, J., & Ratican, J. (2023). Life, death, and AI: Exploring digital necromancy in popular culture, ethical considerations, technological limitations, and the pet cemetery conundrum. _Metaverse, 4_(1).**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.54517/m.v4i1.2166

Published: June 2023.

Why it matters here: this paper directly addresses digital reconstruction of people, the limits of available personal data, accurate personality representation, embodiment, authenticity, consent, and the social and ethical problems created by interactive replicas.

Related records: PRJ-022 Personal Cognitive Digital Twin / JayTwin; PRJ-035 Simulating a 1990s Comic Creator; RQ-001 Persistent Artificial Identity.

### PUB-004: Synthesizing Sentience

**Ratican, J., Hutson, J., & Plate, D. (2023). Synthesizing Sentience: Integrating large language models and autonomous agents for emulating human cognitive complexity. _Journal of Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning and Data Science, 1_(4), 135-141.**

Published: October 20, 2023.

Why it matters here: this is the clearest published ancestor of the modular cognitive-architecture work. It explores the integration of LLMs and autonomous agents to approximate multiple aspects of human cognitive complexity rather than asking one model to perform every function.

Related records: PRJ-032 Modular Cognitive Agents; PRJ-016 Ensemble; PRJ-028 Sidecar Self-Core; PRJ-001 DUCK.

### PUB-005: Individuality and the Collective in AI Agents

**Hutson, J., & Ratican, J. (2023). Individuality and the collective in AI agents: Explorations of shared consciousness and digital homunculi in the metaverse for cultural heritage. _Metaverse, 4_(2).**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.54517/met.v4i2.2276

Published: November 2023.

Why it matters here: this paper explicitly places individuality, collective cognition, shared substrates, digital homunculi, and the representation of self into the research record. The later PEMA, multi-organism, substrate, and identity work can therefore be read partly as attempts to turn a broad philosophical and architectural question into causal experiments.

Related records: RQ-001 Persistent Artificial Identity; RQ-005 Emergent Ecology; PRJ-004 PEMA; PRJ-032 Modular Cognitive Agents.

### PUB-006: Leveraging Generative Agents

**Hutson, J., & Ratican, J. (2023). Leveraging generative agents: Autonomous AI with simulated personas for interactive simulacra and collaborative research. _Journal of Innovation and Technology_.**

Published: December 2023.

Why it matters here: the paper proposes autonomous AI agents with simulated personas and specialized expertise working together in a decentered collaborative system. Persona simulation, emotional simulation, autonomous agents, and heterogeneous expertise all anticipate later work on agent individuality and multi-agent organization.

Related records: PRJ-016 Ensemble; PRJ-032 Modular Cognitive Agents; RQ-005 Emergent Ecology.

### PUB-007: Essence as Algorithm

**Hutson, J., Ratican, J., & Biri, C. (2023). Essence as Algorithm: Public perceptions of AI-powered avatars of real people. _DS Journal of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics, 1_(2), 1-14.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.59232/AIR-V1I2P101

Published: December 2023.

Why it matters here: this work studies perceptions of AI-powered representations of real people and therefore bears directly on what later projects call fidelity, digital twins, synthetic identity, and the gap between resemblance and persistent person-like continuity.

Related records: PRJ-022 Personal Cognitive Digital Twin / JayTwin; RQ-001 Persistent Artificial Identity; RQ-006 Artificiality and Believability.

### PUB-008: Beyond the Pixelated Mirror

**Coble, K., Ratican, J., & Hutson, J. (2023). Beyond the pixelated mirror: Understanding avatar identity and its impact on in-game advertising and consumer behavior. _Metaverse, 4_(2).**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.54517/m.v4i2.2377

Why it matters here: this paper focuses on avatar identity, idealized selves, embodiment, and the relationship between virtual representation and behavior. It is adjacent to the artificial-character work because it treats digital embodiment as psychologically meaningful rather than purely cosmetic.

Related records: embodiment and avatar identity; PRJ-021 Pooka; RQ-001.

### PUB-009: Use of AI to Recreate and Repatriate Lost, Destroyed or Stolen Paintings

**O'Brien, C. E., Hutson, J., Olsen, T., & Ratican, J. (2023). Use of AI to recreate and repatriate lost, destroyed or stolen paintings: The 1785 Parisian Salon case study. _Journal of Innovation and Technology_.**

Why it matters here: this work is not an artificial-life paper, but it contributes to a recurring methodological concern in the later persona research: reconstruction from incomplete evidence, fidelity to historical source material, and the danger of generative interpolation being mistaken for recovered truth.

Related records: PRJ-022 Digital Twin; PRJ-035 1990s Comic Creator; provenance and epistemic fidelity.

## 2024: from conceptual identity questions toward reconstruction and adaptive systems

### PUB-010: Digital Resurrection of Historical Figures

**Hutson, J., Huffman, P., & Ratican, J. (2024). Digital resurrection of historical figures: A case study on Mary Sibley through customized ChatGPT. _Metaverse_.**

Why it matters here: this is especially important to the journal. The study used a substantial autobiographical corpus, including Mary Sibley's diaries, to construct a conversational historical representation intended to reproduce voice and tone.

This provides a concrete published predecessor to the later Digital Twin, simulated-human, biography-development, and developmental-attractor work. It also exposes the distinction between documentary reconstruction and a genuinely persistent developmental individual.

Related records: PRJ-022 Personal Cognitive Digital Twin / JayTwin; PRJ-035 Simulating a 1990s Comic Creator; PRJ-006 Kurzweil Brain Experiments; RQ-001 and RQ-002.

### PUB-011: Advancing Sentiment Analysis Through Emotionally-Agnostic Text Mining

**Ratican, J., & Hutson, J. (2024). Advancing sentiment analysis through emotionally-agnostic text mining in large language models. _Journal of Biosensors and Bioelectronics Research_.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.47363/JBBER/2024(2)118

Published: May 2024.

Why it matters here: this paper deliberately removes human physiological assumptions from text-only affect analysis and proposes text-native measures of emotional congruence. That substrate-sensitive stance strongly resembles the later research principle that an artificial organism should not be forced to imitate biological internals merely because the resulting labels sound human.

Related records: PRJ-033 6DE; PRJ-020 Developmental and Cognitive Theory Notebook; substrate-appropriate cognition.

### PUB-012: Simulacra and Historical Fidelity

**Olsen, T., Hutson, J., O'Brien, C., & Ratican, J. (2024). Simulacra and historical fidelity in digital recreation of lost cultural heritage: Reconstituting period materialities for the period eye. _Arts & Communication_.**

Why it matters here: this work reinforces the distinction between reconstruction, simulation, interpretation, and fidelity to source evidence. Those epistemic concerns later become explicit in World Authority, belief ledgers, provenance-aware memory, and first-person access controls.

Related records: PRJ-025 Project Janus; PRJ-009 Persona Engine; RQ-004 Subjective Access.

### PUB-013: Adaptive Worlds

**Ratican, J., & Hutson, J. (2024). Adaptive Worlds: Generative AI in game design and future of gaming, and interactive media. _ISRG Journal of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences_.**

Published: September 6, 2024.

Why it matters here: the paper discusses real-time generation, player-driven variation, adaptive content, persistent interactive media, AI-written scripts and avatars, and increasingly generative game systems.

Related records: generative environments used for developmental organisms; PEMA worlds; Bicentennial Man; game-world simulation.

### PUB-014: Video Game Development 3.0

**Ratican, J., & Hutson, J. (2024). Video game development 3.0: AI-driven collaborative co-creation. _Metaverse_.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.54517/m2904

Published: December 2024.

Why it matters here: this paper treats adaptive NPCs, dynamic content, LLMs, neural systems, and AI-driven creation as part of a broader game-development architecture. It is especially relevant to the practical bridge between artificial-life research and game-engine implementation.

### PUB-015: Enhancing Assessment and Feedback in Game Design Programs

**Hutson, J., Fulcher, B., & Ratican, J. (2024). Enhancing assessment and feedback in game design programs: Leveraging generative AI for efficient and meaningful evaluation. _International Journal of Educational Research and Innovation_.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.46661/ijeri.11038

Published: December 2024.

Why it matters here: this is primarily pedagogical rather than artificial-life research. It belongs in the complete publication record but does not need to be treated as an ancestor of the organism architecture.

## 2026: persistence becomes an explicit systems architecture problem

### PUB-016: Hybrid Server-AI Architecture for Persistent Generative Game Worlds

**Ratican, J., & Hutson, J. (2026). Hybrid Server-AI architecture for persistent generative game worlds: Achieving scalable, consistent, and low-latency interactive environments. _ISAR Journal of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences, 4_(2), 43-52.**

Published: February 2026.

Why it matters here: this paper has unusually direct overlap with the later artificial-life research. It separates deterministic authoritative state from a state-aware generative layer and introduces mechanisms for persistence, causal coherence, consistency, and identity continuity across sessions.

The architecture includes an authoritative server backend, a state-aware generative layer, a neural post-processing layer, a content-addressable generation ledger, and a generative determinism envelope.

This is closely aligned with later principles such as World Authority, canonical state, renderer non-authority, persistent subject state, and the separation of generative expression from the system that owns truth.

Related records: PRJ-009 Persona Engine; PRJ-025 Project Janus; PRJ-001 DUCK; RQ-004 Subjective Access; persistent-world infrastructure.

## Broader adjacent published work

Other coauthored publications in the Lindenwood record address metaverse infrastructure, virtual real estate, AI-assisted cultural heritage reconstruction, avatar identity, AI-supported game-design education, generative game development, and digital restoration.

They should remain in the scholarly bibliography even where they do not directly generate an artificial-life project, because they establish the surrounding research context in which the persistent-character program developed.

The most important continuity is that the present experiments did not begin from an isolated interest in "AI consciousness." The published work repeatedly concerns identity, simulation fidelity, emotion, modularity, persistent environments, embodiment, individual versus collective organization, and the distinction between generative output and the underlying state being represented.

## Source policy

For publication metadata and article interpretation, use sources in this order:

1. Lindenwood University Digital Commons institutional record and full-text copy.
2. Publisher page and DOI record.
3. ResearchGate profile or upload for discovery and cross-checking.
4. Conversation memory only when no documentary source can be recovered.

If a later journal note makes a substantive claim about what a paper argued, it should link to the institutional record or full text rather than relying on this bibliography alone.
