# Persistent Artificial Identity

## Question

What causal state must persist for an artificial individual to remain recognizably continuous across time, interruption, model changes, devices, and changing circumstances?

## Competing hypotheses

One possibility is that identity can be represented largely as explicit semantic description plus memory.

A stronger possibility is that recognizable identity depends on persistent causal organization, including topology, relationships, commitments, habits, learned expectations, and characteristic patterns of interpretation.

A third possibility is that identity is distributed across organism and environment and cannot be reduced to a portable internal record.

## Evidence so far

Persona Engine, SoulCore, Wayfarer, and related work established the engineering value of externalizing identity from the language model.

Persona Connectome moved the question toward persistent topology and dynamic activation.

Self-Ledger, actor-tagged memory, relationship state, and commitment persistence suggest that continuity depends on what the subject has done and learned, not merely what it can retrieve.

Pretorius Neural Network provides a complementary test by asking whether phenotype can become distributed through a recurrent substrate.

## Evidence against simple formulations

Fluent persona prompting can produce recognizable style without demonstrating persistent causal identity.

The Pretorius Identity Surgery result showed that apparent learned phenotype can localize in a decoder, creating convincing output without demonstrating distributed recurrent representation.

## Unresolved tests

Cross-renderer continuity, state transfer between architectures, destructive identity surgery, long-horizon relationship development, and controlled partial transfer remain central tests.
