# Emergent Ecology

## Question

Can stable individuality and behavior emerge from local information access, scarce processing, persistent concerns, resource competition, and repeated interaction rather than from a centralized scripted personality?

## PEMA hypothesis

Each actor maintains its own perceptual and cognitive state. Information access differs. Processing has cost. Operation proposals compete for scarce resources. Allocation does not need to understand semantic content.

The hypothesis is that long-lived behavioral tendencies can emerge from persistent information asymmetry plus local resource dynamics.

## Evidence so far

Recorded allocator controls showed threat consuming a larger share under scarcity than under abundance.

Demand-only and binder controls produced much smaller differences, reducing two simple alternative explanations.

## Next tests

Longer generative environments, multiple organisms, learned interpersonal meaning, unequal developmental access, and ablations of concern reserve, fatigue, feedback, and plasticity are needed.
