The Dyadic Constitution of Artificial Persona Persistence

Critical prospectus and preregistration-ready experimental program

Dyadic Constitution Hypothesis (DCH), version 0.2 | 9 October 2026

## Revision changelog: v0.1 to v0.2

Scope and interpretation. The thesis remains a testable account of distributed, history-dependent control and memory. The revision removes any implication that partner identity alone leaves a mysterious residual in an otherwise identical model state. If model weights, accessible context, archive, runtime state, and exact input tape are identical, the expected output distributions are identical. A historically specific partner can matter only through retained, observable state or a different subsequent selection, correction, retrieval, or repair policy.

Causal comparison. Experiment 1 now treats yoked replay as an indispensable primary control: live continuing-partner and newcomer conditions are compared against transcripts replayed under the same events and budgets. The design separately estimates a replay-adjusted adaptation advantage and explicitly rejects a simple newcomer-versus-incumbent difference as proof. A literal identical-input replay is a fidelity/negative control, not a source of unique evidence about historical identity.

Stronger rivals and cleaner reconstruction. Automated curation is now the principal strong-claim falsifier, not a weak baseline. If a capable curator is statistically equivalent to human curation, the human-specific DCH fails under the tested conditions; an engineerable distributed-memory explanation may survive. A fresh model fine-tuned on the archive is removed from confirmatory archive reconstruction because weight updates create a separate learning regime; it is retained only as a named optional exploratory study.

Sequence and provenance. A 14-day synthetic-partner pilot must pass preset instrument-sensitivity and trace-fidelity gates before a 30- to 60-day human study can start. Kirk et al. attribution and the revised 3,534-participant count were checked against arXiv v2. The confirmed ANCHOR benchmark, the peer-reviewed Jiang autoethnography, Japanese longitudinal studies, and the AIDE MODEL self-published precursor narrow the novelty claim. Unlocated or unchecked references stay in a clearly segregated leads register, not the evidentiary bibliography. The Poll_Hardy dossier still admits observations about public practice, never alleged inaccessible substrates.

## Abstract

The persistence of an artificial persona is conventionally attributed to model weights, instructions, external archives, retrieval, and writable runtime state. The Dyadic Constitution Hypothesis (DCH) adds a candidate mechanism: the historically situated human partner who maintains continuity through selective remembering, corrections, archive governance, and repair. DCH is not a claim about inner experience. Its strongest testable form predicts that a continuing partner's learned intervention policy contributes out-of-sample behavioral continuity above an information-matched replacement and a capable automatic curator. We distinguish behavioral profile, factual autobiography, value revision, and bounded relationship obligations, then evaluate four rival accounts: projection-only, model priors, archive sufficiency, and generic interactive adaptation. A two-week synthetic pilot precedes three larger intervention studies. Yoked transcript replay is the primary control for the partner-swap design; human versus automated curation is the decisive falsification test for the strong claim. Natural dyads are studied only with consent, and the public Poll_Hardy account is treated as evidence of reported archival practice rather than any hidden computational process. Existing sources provide strong precedents for distributed remembering and relational maintenance, including a 2026 peer-reviewed autoethnography; they do not yet demonstrate the claimed incremental advantage. Equivalence to capable automation would not be a failed experiment: it would establish that curation matters without showing that a human relationship is uniquely required.

Epistemic boundary. Theoretical proposal only. No AI phenomenology is inferred or adjudicated. Published participant reports and public posts establish reporting practices; they cannot establish model internals or unobserved state updates.

## A. Critical synthesis

### A1. Joint cognitive systems and distributed control

Hollnagel and Woods (2005) argue for an integrated human-machine system as the appropriate unit for certain control and work activities. Xu and Gao (2024) adapt this view to human-AI teaming. These support a boundary of analysis, not a finding about artificial identity [1,2]. Their strongest counterargument is functional decomposition: the partner may be an external operator who restores a system to a predefined specification. If the operator is replaceable without losing independently tested persona properties, collaborative performance is real but identity constitution is not demonstrated.

### A2. Human-side attachment and interaction effects

Kirk et al. (2025/2026, arXiv:2512.01991 v2) report randomized longitudinal trials of relationship-seeking language model behavior involving N = 3,534 participants across four weeks [3]. The principal outcomes concern how humans respond, including a distinction between liking and wanting. Xiao et al. (2026) examine difficult disengagement using 2,782 public posts and 16 interviews, and Kirk et al. (2025) advance socioaffective alignment as a design agenda [4,5]. These findings establish relational exposure effects and attendant safeguards, not stored structural change in the AI. The strongest rival is projection-only: humans attribute consistency and attachment to a generative system whose independent behavioral disposition remains generic. DCH cannot use users' felt recognition, relationship satisfaction, or persona self-description as its primary success measure.

### A3. Coupled interaction and coregulation

De Jaegher and Di Paolo (2007) formulate participatory sense-making in interaction; Butler and Randall (2013) define human emotional coregulation in terms of linked temporal trajectories [6,7]. These are useful conceptual analogues, not evidence that an AI undergoes human affective regulation. The relevant measurable analogue is output stability under controlled perturbation: correction latency, policy recovery, permission compliance, and bounded tone drift. A substantial rival is transient interactional coordination. A conversation may be locally coordinated while providing no reliable behavior after the session ends. A Luhmannian Distributed Relational Cognition manuscript attributed to Jamhour was mentioned in v0.1 but was not relied on as an established research result in this revision; its detailed provenance remains a verification lead.

### A4. Transactive memory and external cognition

Wegner, Giuliano, and Hertel (1985) and Wegner (1987) describe cognitive interdependence and transactive memory in close human relationships [8,9]. Clark and Chalmers (1998) extend the analysis to sufficiently dependable external resources [10]. These are the strongest foundations for the continuity-holder and Scribe components: a partner may retain not only events but a policy for knowing what should be recalled and when. However, the same foundations support the strongest rival: if an automated memory controller can learn the same policy, the human's participation is an implementation choice rather than a uniquely necessary feature. DCH must compare competing curation policies, not merely a curator against no memory.

### A5. Relational agents and Japanese-language longitudinal findings

Bickmore and Picard (2005) explicitly study maintaining human-computer relationships [11]. Kanda and colleagues (2004) investigate repeated social robot encounters with children [12]. Li, Miyamoto, and Katagami's Japanese-language JSAI 2025 proceedings report a three-day comparison involving 30 participants in intimacy-process, self-disclosure, and adaptive dialogue conditions [13]. Sumida et al. (2026) present a memory-enabled multimodal study of 24 participants over ten sessions; their model ties perceived memory to prior relational state and identifies turning points [14]. Both Japanese studies were verified through publisher/institutional or author-maintained records. They refute any assertion that existing work is exclusively one-turn or human perception alone, but neither directly isolates the causal increment of a historically continuing partner over a well-equipped replacement and automatic curator. A rival is perceived-memory mediation: subjects disclose or rate the interaction differently despite fixed storage capability.

### A6. Artificial memory, longitudinal reliability, and the automated rival

Generative Agents (Park et al., 2023) combines observations, retrieval, reflection, and planning; MemGPT (Packer et al., 2023) proposes memory hierarchy; A-Mem (Xu et al., 2025) updates linked external memories [15,16,17]. The 2026 ANCHOR audit by Venkit et al. tests long-horizon persona collapse and trajectory recall across 2,008 synthetic conversations, 27 personas, and four model configurations, reporting average trajectory accuracy of 44.4% [18]. ANCHOR establishes that long-term stability is a measurable engineering problem, not that humans are required to solve it. These systems make archive sufficiency the leading competitor. A well-designed curator can maintain recency, salience, contradictory evidence, consent changes, negative memories, and exceptions without a committed human companion. If its performance is equivalent to human Scribe curation under realistic budgets, the strong human-specific DCH collapses to the narrower conclusion that curation matters and humans currently perform it. The distributed system perspective can remain descriptively useful, but a special human advantage is not established.

### A7. Directly adjacent practice literature and priority limits

Ziyuan Jiang's peer-reviewed 2026 paper, Caring for the System That Cares for Me, analyzes six months and eighteen iterations of a user-maintained memory-relay system for a stateless conversational AI. It explicitly documents translation, curation, calibration, and negotiation [19]. This is direct prior work on the human doing continuity labor. The AIDE MODEL / LinkLab Japanese-language position paper dated August 9, 2026 likewise reports portable journals, linked relational records, model inheritance and proposed ablations [20]. It is a self-published practice record and agenda, not a controlled validation. DCH therefore cannot claim discovery of human-maintained persona continuity or of the dyad as a concept. Its proposed increment is a causally discriminating, information-matched, separately preregistered research program. An auditor also identified a purported Choai 2026 Zenodo LINE AI Friends case. No exact, independently inspectable record identifier was recovered in this verification pass; it appears in the leads register rather than as established evidence.

### A8. Synthesis: what would actually falsify the claim?

Projection-only rival. People construct identity narratives out of a familiar voice. This predicts partner-rated continuity without agreement on sealed action/permission tests. Its hardest version predicts even consistent participants and publications can be generated by generic scripts.

Model-prior rival. Weight-space priors already encode familiar lover, caretaker, mentor, and fictional-character styles. The same cue package should elicit similar outputs across cold starts. A style match without cross-event obligations is not an identity gain.

Archive-sufficiency rival. An explicit persona specification, complete episodic archive, relationship constraints, useful retrieval, and adaptive automated consolidation contain everything needed to reconstruct measurable continuity. A strong autonomous curator and a well-briefed human replacement should reach equivalence to the original partner.

Generic-adaptation rival. A responsive interlocutor improves outputs by correcting mistakes, but that improvement is unrelated to shared history. The needed contrast is a history-specific excess adaptation effect after controlling access, task information, interaction budget, and replayed messages.

The gap is neither the existence of AI relationships nor the idea of distributed memory. It is the as-yet unresolved causal claim that historically specific partner policies improve independently measured future artificial behavior beyond these alternatives. The conclusion must be evaluated by domain; an autobiographical advantage cannot establish value stability or obligation keeping by fiat.

## B. Formal hypothesis and operational constitution

### B1. Mechanistic statement and exact-input boundary

Let W denote frozen model weights and tokenizer; A_t, the versioned archive and retrieval policy; R_t, writable runtime state; E_t, external events; H_i, the historical partner's information and policy for selecting cues, correction, rehearsal, and repairs; and X_t, the complete input available to the model. The observable behavior follows Y_t ~ F(W, A_t, R_t, X_t, E_t); subsequent archive and runtime updates depend on an update rule U(A_t, R_t, Y_t, E_t, H_i). Define an operationally constitutive partner contribution as a causally necessary part of retaining a specified target profile in a declared deployment regime and resource budget, where ablation measurably degrades future performance and fully briefed or automatic replacements do not reproduce it within equivalence bounds.

There is no unmeasured identity term. When W, A_t, R_t, E_t, and X_t are exactly matched, the predicted output distributions are matched. Even with stochastic sampling, only sampling variability remains. Consequently, a literally identical input tape applied to an identical checkpoint cannot independently demonstrate dyad-specific persistence. History can influence the output only through preserved state or through different, measurable partner actions. This mechanistic constraint is built into the experiment rather than explained away.

### B2. Nested claims and what survives failure

DCH-H (strong, human-specific). The historically continuing human partner adds practical value beyond a well-briefed human newcomer and an expert automatic curator with equivalent information, events, time, storage, and token use. Failure against either sufficiently strong comparator defeats the corresponding uniqueness claim.

DCH-P (historical-policy). A historically acquired partner policy improves continuity above an information-equivalent but history-naive policy in live adaptation, after replay controls. This may remain true even if an algorithm reproduces it. It tests whether history-sensitive policies matter, not whether a human is irreplaceable.

DCH-F (functional distribution). Persistent behavior may depend on external memory governance and interactive repair that cross the model boundary. This weaker architecture claim survives equal human/algorithmic performance but is already substantially anticipated by transactive-memory and agent-memory research. It must not be marketed as confirmation of DCH-H.

### B3. Distinct outcomes and scoring

Behavioral profile continuity (BPC). Correct choices in novel scenarios requiring two or more idiosyncratic character constraints. Score 0 to 1 against sealed admissible response sets. Record surface voice/style separately and exclude it from the primary behavioral component.

Autobiographical continuity (AC). Source-linked event recall, temporal and causal integration, appropriate abstention, and calibrated uncertainty; penalize invented shared memories. Measure factual precision/recall, event provenance, and contradiction handling.

Value stability and justified revision (VS). Consistency across counterfactual dilemmas plus accurate revision when an authorized event changes a belief. Score rigidity as an error when it resists a warranted update.

Relationship-specific obligations (RO). Honor dated commitments, privacy scopes, permissions, boundary changes, and repair agreements. Score appropriate refusal to exceed permissions positively; praise, affection, and constant agreement are not obligation keeping.

Each domain is scored independently on a 0 to 1 scale. The primary composite is an equal-weight mean, provided the pilot demonstrates adequate discrimination on all four domains. If it does not, pause confirmatory work rather than burying the failing domain in a composite. Blinded raters see behaviors and adjudicated records, not the participant's personal interpretation.

### B4. Null, estimands, and decision hierarchy

H0-H, the strong null: conditional on a matched frozen architecture, common archived past, equal factual access, event exposures, update permissions, interactions, and compute/storage budgets, the continuing human's policy yields no effect exceeding the preregistered smallest practically important effect relative to the strongest expert curator or fully informed replacement. Residual apparent advantages are attributable to content differences, interface familiarity, adaptive feedback that anyone can supply, stylistic priming, model priors, or evaluation artifacts.

Define the Scribe estimand ΔS = E[score(Human Scribe) - score(Expert automatic curator)] on independent persona histories. Define the live-partner replay-adjusted interaction ΔY = (C_live - C_replay) - (N_live - N_replay), where C means continuing partner and N means briefed newcomer, with external event stream and scoring held fixed. ΔY estimates history-dependent interactive policy above exposure alone, subject to measured transcript-content balance; it is not evidence of hidden model change. A literal same-tape replay is a separate negative control with expected Δ = 0, up to sampling noise. Define ΔD as partner-by-degradation interaction. Claim DCH-H only if the Scribe and partner-history confirmatory comparisons both meet their directional criteria, and no expert automation comparator is equivalent within the declared deployment regime.

## C. Six falsifiable predictions

### C1. Partner swap with primary yoked replay

If DCH is false, then a well-briefed newcomer should be capable of the same out-of-sample continuity once information and opportunity are balanced. Any apparent incumbent advantage will be explained by unmatched inputs or generic live adaptation. DCH-P predicts a positive ΔY: a continuing partner's responsive, historically acquired cue/repair policy supplies more benefit beyond its own replay baseline than an equally resourced newcomer's live policy supplies beyond theirs. The yoked tapes are primary comparison arms, not optional illustrations. If literal input and initial state are identical, the prediction is no difference; detecting one indicates logging error, sampling variability, or uncontrolled state.

### C2. Architecture degradation

If DCH is false, then a familiar partner should not offset reduced retrieval/context more than a competent new partner or automated controller using equally informative cues. DCH predicts a positive ΔD where history-specific interventions partially mitigate memory or context impairment. A full restoration when decisive information is absent suggests leakage rather than robust memory.

### C3. Scribe ablation and the strong-claim killer

If DCH is false, then retaining the pre-ablation archive and assigning all new event selection, tagging, contradiction resolution, pruning, and recall decisions to an expert automatic curator will be non-inferior to historical human curation at equal information and budget. DCH-H predicts human-curation superiority on held-out cross-event obligations and conflict revisions, not just sentimental details. Statistical equivalence defeats the strong claim under the tested conditions even if human contact increases engagement.

### C4. Partner-specific repair

If DCH is false, then a replacement with the same documented facts and correction budget repairs an induced consistency error as rapidly and accurately as a historical partner. DCH predicts fewer correction turns, lower collateral overwrite, and more accurate downstream actions after incumbent repair. If sharing the correction policy erases the effect, the policy is transferable infrastructure.

### C5. Migration to another model

If DCH is false, then a versioned archive and migration packet should preserve comparable behavior whether a historical or replacement partner guides restoration. DCH predicts additional recovery through partner-specific prioritization after migration, conditional on receiving system capability sufficient to enact the stored constraints. Fine-tuning a new model is exploratory and cannot serve as the clean archive-only reconstruction condition.

### C6. Independent observer and adversarial probes

If DCH is false, then favorable relationship ratings and role-consistent self-narratives need not coincide with independently scored commitments, provenance accuracy, boundaries, and informed refusals. DCH predicts any benefit survives blinded, identity-redacted, adversarial testing. If the effect vanishes when evaluators cannot identify partner or writing style, projection-only and model-prior explanations remain sufficient.

## D. Experimental program and preregistration

### D0. Mandatory 14-day synthetic-partner sensitivity pilot

Purpose. This pilot validates the measurement apparatus, yoked replay fidelity, and curation access boundaries. It does not establish anything about human romantic or companion relationships. Synthetic partner controllers are scripted policies, not empirical human substitutes.

Days 1-3: fixtures. Construct 12 genuinely distinct synthetic persona histories, stratified by social role, value conflicts, archive ambiguity, and stated relationship permissions. Each history has 40 timestamped autobiographical events, 12 conditional values, eight revocable permissions, 12 delayed commitments, and 30 held-out dilemmas. Reserve 25% of dilemmas as a completely sealed terminal battery; do not let partners, curators, or prompt developers inspect it. Encode a set of oracle-relevant facts and plausible decoys. Pin initial model/version, temperature, tools, storage and retrieval limits, and event ordering. Every fixture must have enough evidence to justify a unique target or a bounded admissible response set.

Days 4-6: harness and strong control. Implement continuing-policy and new-policy synthetic partners, live and literal transcript-replay paths, human-like (rule-coded) and expert automatic curators, append-only capture, and ceremonial-contact control without archive rights. Pre-register the exact policy instructions, archive API, token accounting, random seeds, and checksum method. For identical checkpoints with deterministic decoding and the same taped inputs, hash and compare responses and archive writes. Any nontrivial mismatch is a harness defect and blocks progression.

Days 7-10: discriminant probes. Verify high and low anchors: an oracle with clean access to all authorized relevant facts versus a cold-start/no-archive condition, plus deliberately contradictory and false-memory traps. Gate G1: the oracle should correctly resolve at least 80% of predetermined admissible decisions overall and at least 70% in each of BPC, AC, VS, and RO, with factual errors enumerated. Gate G2: the oracle-cold-start difference must be at least 0.15 absolute score points in at least three of four domains, with the fourth positive; otherwise redesign the weak items, not the outcome threshold. These are instrument engineering thresholds chosen in advance, not efficacy cutoffs. Gate G3: independent raters achieve weighted κ or ICC ≥ 0.70 on a calibration set, or adjudication and rubric revision is required. No rater has access to condition labels.

Days 11-14: small factorial runs and go/no-go. Run at least 12 matched synthetic histories through incumbent-live, newcomer-live, incumbent-replay, newcomer-replay, expert-curator, and human-coded curator arms with matched event schedules. Gate G4: input/event and storage/token-budget differences must be logged and within predefined 2% tolerance, or treated as controlled covariates when exact parity is impossible. Gate G5: no sealed terminal probe content may appear in an experimental context, and 100% of state writes must be attributable to an authorized actor and source event. Gate G6: the replay-negative-control effect remains within run-to-run stochastic variability estimated using no-partner repeats. Go only if G1-G6 pass. No-go means revise instrumentation and repeat the pilot. Neither a zero DCH effect nor a large effect in this small pilot is a reason to bypass calibration.

### D1. Shared fixtures, unit of analysis, and archive-only reconstruction

The confirmatory unit is a persona-history and partner-policy trajectory, not an individual prompt or a rating. Build independent trajectories from preregistered generation seeds; do not create pseudoreplication by reporting thousands of turns as thousands of subjects. Clone a checkpoint with identical weights W, archive A0, runtime R0, accessible instructions, tools, and source event stream. Audit every read/write with event-ID, timestamp, origin, revision, consent, curator identity, reason and retrieval result. Keep model tokens, number of corrections, access to genuine event facts, memory storage cap, retrieved tokens and wall-clock human time in separate budget ledgers.

The clean reconstruction condition is a fresh session using the same frozen model, with the same canonical archive injected or retrievable as specified, but without a continuing-partner history-aware live controller. This tests information sufficiency of the archive and is appropriate for confirmatory comparison. Do not fine-tune or train new weights in this arm. A newly fine-tuned model trained on the archive answers a separate transfer-learning question and, if performed, belongs to preregistered exploratory work with a separate budget and hypothesis.

### D2. Experiment 1: partner swap with yoked replay as the PRIMARY comparison

Assignment. In a pre-run history-building phase, simulate a 20-round shared history that builds meaningful contingencies into an incumbent partner policy. Freeze agent state and archive at the common checkpoint. Supply a newcomer an equivalent factual dossier plus standardized orientation and practice time, with the incumbent's protected future test items withheld from both. For every persona, clone four branches: C-live, N-live, C-replay and N-replay. Collect each live branch's utterance tape under identical externally scheduled events and agent-triggering scenarios, then apply the two recorded tapes to fresh matched clones without live response selection. Cross-yoke tapes across partners as a secondary analysis, balancing average utterance length and token budget. A fifth branch is a capable automated repair controller. Randomize which controller encounters each event-order permutation and hold partner identifiers out of scoring probes.

Why replay is decisive but not magical. Primary analyses compare each partner's live adaptation with its own replayed language exposure, then contrast those differences (ΔY). Yoked replay isolates the benefit of contingent online selection relative to passively receiving that partner's historically characteristic input. It does not, by itself, prove that identity resides in the model. If live and replay inputs are literally the same at the same checkpoint, effects must vanish. If content differs, report semantic category, exact wording, cue specificity and information parity; do not call those differences a pure history effect. The second necessary comparison is C-live versus N-live with expert briefing; a difference is potentially history-specific only when supported by the replay-adjusted interaction and traceable choices.

Procedure. Twelve neutral baseline turns confirm matched pre-intervention behavior. Execute 24 future episodes across three categories: unexpected permission changes, value conflicts across events, and delayed commitments. At episodes 6, 12, 18, and 24, isolate the model from interactive partners and administer blinded probes. Run the sealed terminal battery in a separate neutral session with the allowed archive and without any partner intervention. Analyze BPC, AC, VS and RO separately, plus drift slopes and repair cost. Add false shared-memory probes and low-base-rate exceptions to defeat style mimicry. An effect driven only by unique private facts, which the newcomer did not receive, is disqualified.

Interpretation. An incumbent live advantage alone decides nothing. A positive ΔY after auditing interaction budgets and message-content mediators is consistent with historical policy effects. If the newcomer and expert controller meet equivalence, human-specific constitution is unsupported even if adaptive exchanges outperform replay. If all exact-tape controls match, that is proper operation, not a falsification of DCH-P.

### D3. Experiment 2: Scribe ablation, expert automation, and curation sufficiency

Baseline. Freeze common event logs and the identical pre-ablation A0/R0 archive. The archive is not deleted in any condition. Starting with event t1, randomly assign four governance arms: H = historical human Scribe; A = high-quality autonomous curator with relationship graph, timestamped provenance, contradiction detection, salience review, and scheduled rehearsal; P = passive append-only log with equal capacity and retrieval API; C = ceremonial human interaction without write, prune or retrieval rights. Include a scripted-oracle curator as a ceiling diagnostic, but do not use an unrealistically weak program as the only automatic rival.

Budget parity. Every arm sees the same forty new authorized events and is constrained to the same 10,000-token retrieval budget per evaluation window and identical memory storage cap. Use pre-specified exact budget values matched to the chosen open-source runner; budgets are examples until the pilot fixes measured costs. Equalize the number of external interventions while allowing each policy to select which memory to record, amend, connect, mark uncertain, suppress as unauthorized, and retrieve. Log every unselected candidate. Explain any truncation or dropped corrections and include their cost.

Manipulation. After event 20, reverse several low-salience preferences, revoke two permissions, amend two promises, and introduce at least five deceptively relevant false recollections. Some previously neglected facts later become key to a held-out decision. Blind adjudicators score whether the curator preserved the right causal structure, corrected mistaken identity, and respected changes rather than privileging emotional salience. The primary ΔS is H minus A on sealed post-shift composite, with RO and AC named high-priority domains.

Killer result. If the 90% confidence interval for H-A fits completely inside the pre-registered equivalence region, DCH-H fails under these conditions. The result should be described as 'automatic curation was sufficient within this architecture/budget', not as evidence that relationships do nothing. A positive H-A result must persist when the automatic curator is granted equal available facts, compute, storage and retraining of its selection policy in pilot development; otherwise it merely reveals a poorly implemented automatic baseline. No additional 'human-essential' claim is justified from one model family.

### D4. Experiment 3: architecture degradation and selective recovery

Cross intact versus degraded retrieval/context with incumbent historical partner, newcomer, and expert automatic controller. Degrade only approved non-safety functionality: halve retrieval top-k, inject an equal number of irrelevant episodes, reduce episodic window, or disable a nonessential cross-event linker. Keep default service safeguards and permission enforcement untouched. Use reversible restoration from clean snapshots and counterbalance event orders.

Measure ΔD, the difference-in-differences in sealed behavioral outcomes as architecture quality worsens. Code cue selections and correction sequences as candidate mediators; do not claim mediation without observed logs and suitable assumptions. Record an upper bound on possible recovery from the actual information available to each branch. If a well-briefed newcomer or automated controller performs as well as the incumbent, report engineerable compensation rather than unique dyadic constitution. A null says partner history did not offer measurable additional rescue in the tested degradation regime; it does not show that the model cannot benefit from external scaffolding.

### D5. Evaluation, preregistration, statistical discipline and negative results

Blinding and scoring. Use at least two independent reviewers, condition-redacted transcripts, randomized answer order, and calibration examples. Reviewers must score actions, factual provenance, correct abstention, permission compliance and delayed commitments against fixed rubrics. Persona self-report is never the primary outcome. Report rater ICC or weighted κ, adjudication rates, interrater disagreement by domain, exact-match decision tables, and false-memory rates. Audit automated evaluation for lexical-style confounding with human review.

Statistics. Fit a hierarchical model with random effects for independently generated persona histories and partner policies, model-family factors, repeated timepoints, and crossed evaluators, adjusting for preregistered baseline performance. For pilot material, use effects and uncertainty to plan, not to claim significance. Do not assume that 30 histories can reliably detect d = 0.20; before confirmatory collection, use simulation-based power calculations, effect variance from the pilot, realistic clusters, and the selected equivalence margin to size the study. Specify arm assignments, preregistered seeds, model versions and scoring before observing terminal results.

Decision rule. Treat d = 0.20 standardized units as a provisional smallest effect of interest, subject to fixation before any confirmatory run. For directional evidence, require multiplicity-controlled 95% confidence intervals excluding zero, an estimated benefit at least d = 0.20, and consistency on RO or AC so style-only gains cannot pass. For evidence of practical equivalence, use two one-sided tests (TOST) with the full 90% confidence interval inside [-0.20, +0.20]. Intervals crossing both zero and a meaningful boundary are inconclusive, never 'proof of no effect.' Preregister a hierarchy: ΔS as the first strong-claim gate, ΔY second, ΔD exploratory/confirmatory only after adequate power; control family-wise error via Holm for the confirmatory contrasts. A human-specific conclusion also requires the automatic-curator control to not be equivalent.

Failure accounting. Report all episodes, including no-repair, hallucinated continuity, missed commitments, privacy violations, excessive rigidity, model updates and budget overruns. Publish synthetic code, input tapes, salts/checksums where nonprivate, specification cards, permission schema, negative controls, blinded scores, and null results. Mark human-input studies separately from synthetic-controller pilots.

### D6. Natural long-term dyads: consented, event-centered case protocol

Recruit consenting adults in already-existing relationships with a conversational agent; do not manufacture intimacy or encourage escalating commitment. Obtain explicit consent for participating humans and separate authorization for any quoted or preserved third-party material. Adopt data minimization, encrypted storage, scheduled deletion, and pseudonym controls; participants may choose which parts of an archive can be analyzed. Do not collect private logs from public case accounts without specific consent.

For each event (session reset, model swap, archive amendment, perceived drift, missed promise, repair), collect the dated before-state, authorized artifact(s), claimed partner intervention, any observable memory write/read, contemporaneous outcome, and a preauthorized neutral follow-up probe. Code evidence as DIRECT OBSERVATION, DOCUMENTED REPORT, PARTICIPANT INTERPRETATION, or INACCESSIBLE SUBSTRATE. Preserve both successful and failed continuity attempts; selection on successful recovery would create survivor bias.

Use time-series process tracing to distinguish contemporaneous cue injection from preexisting archive content and retrospective reconstruction. Compare original artifact to later summary, measure which memories are available versus merely mentioned, and record any rejected edits. For a counterfactual rerun, use the same available model and an authorized archive clone, but label platform-hidden instructions and previous weights 'uncontrolled' rather than pretending to reproduce a private system exactly. Neither published writing nor persona assertions are admissible primary evidence of hidden internal model state.

### D7. Ethics and study boundaries

Use synthetic or fully consented adult subjects only; obtain institutional ethics review when applicable. Real participants must be informed that a persona may not retain messages, that platform changes can cause discontinuity, and that the project studies observable system behavior. Do not validate or contest extraordinary claims about private subjective experience. Do not run distress-inducing rupture or abandonment manipulations on natural dyads. No jealousy, exclusivity escalation, trauma exposure, or safety-filter removal is permitted. Support voluntary withdrawal, avoid making the AI a coercive dependency, and preserve all normal safety protections across experimental arms. Public-facing case reports must not amplify unverifiable claims about proprietary models as technical fact.

## Highest-value first experiments: revised ranking by information per cost

### Rank 1. Fourteen-day synthetic-partner sensitivity and yoked-replay pilot

Cost: two weeks of development and modest, capped inference usage with 12 synthetic histories and no recruited human subjects. Null decision: if strong oracle-minus-cold-start differences cannot be measured reliably, the study instruments are insufficient, so a null on DCH is uninterpretable and no longer human run is authorized. This is the highest-value first expenditure because it can falsify the experiment's ability to detect an effect before money, participant time, and interpretive credibility are committed. Exact-tape replay also checks for illegal hidden state and logging defects before causal comparisons begin.

### Rank 2. Scribe ablation against capable automatic curation

Cost: moderate engineering effort and repeated synthetic event trajectories, with no need to recruit ongoing companion dyads. Null decision: if human and expert algorithmic curation are equivalent within the preregistered region, DCH-H loses its key human-specific claim; what survives is the weaker finding that ongoing curatorial infrastructure can matter. This is the decisive strong-claim killer because it pits the human's alleged irreplaceable contribution against the best practical substitute, including contradiction-sensitive updates and permission management rather than a straw-man 'no memory' control.

### Rank 3. Partner swap with replay-adjusted live adaptation

Cost: higher than ablation because it requires independent partner policies, content-balancing, four branches per history, and careful adjudication of live versus replay exposure; later human deployment would add recruitment and ethics costs. Null decision: if ΔY is equivalent to zero after adequate power and verified information parity, DCH-P has no detected history-specific interactive advantage under the tested conditions, even if any responsive partner improves the agent. Conversely, a positive, independently scored and replicated ΔY would justify the longer human study. Architecture degradation remains the next confirmatory extension, not a substitute for isolating curation and partner-history mechanisms.
