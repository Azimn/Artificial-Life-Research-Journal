# JELLY Jar and Native Jar

## Core research question

Can repeated experience physically reorganize a computational substrate enough to produce persistent, history-dependent internal organization?

## JELLY Jar

JELLY Jar uses local cells, asynchronous events, chemistry-like variables, damage and repair, plasticity, growth, extracellular structure, mutation, and low-level sensing.

The line produced lesion survival and developmental anatomical divergence. Later computational-response divergence was weaker than the anatomical result, which is an important limitation rather than a reason to discard the experiment.

## Native Jar

Native Jar asks a more radical question: what if computation itself supplies the physics?

Candidate primitives include processes, memory pages, copy-on-write inheritance, files, IPC, signals, scheduler time, CPU, RAM, disk, process identifiers, and file descriptors.

The organism would be persistent organization distributed across those resources rather than an application merely running on top of them.

## Program significance

Together these projects establish that substrate research predates the later Latent Organism proposal and forms a coherent lineage of artificial-biological and native-computational substrate experiments.
