# Simulating a 1990s Comic Creator

This March 2025 project asked whether a synthetic creator could develop coherent artistic behavior from a constructed biography rather than simply imitate a writing style.

The simulated person was framed as a 1990s comic developer and writer. Diary-like records, relationships, personal history, references, emotional and internal state, and the user's original comic characters and concepts would form the person's lived creative history.

The intended result was not merely period-authentic prose. The stronger goal was a simulated author whose creative choices were shaped by remembered experience, stable opinions, values, relationships, and a persistent sense of authorship.

The project also explored fine-tuning smaller local models on diaries and personal data, with a reusable pipeline for human-like simulated creators.

This is an important predecessor to later developmental-attractor research. It already distinguishes style imitation from history-shaped behavior, a distinction that becomes central in Kurzweil Brain Experiments, Pretorius biography development, and same-origin divergence tests.
