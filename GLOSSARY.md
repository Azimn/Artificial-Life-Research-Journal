# Working Glossary

## Subject

The continuing artificial individual whose history, limited access, commitments, relationships, and learned changes are treated as causally meaningful. "Subject" does not imply phenomenal consciousness.

## Organism

A continuously running artificial system in which state, needs, perception, action, consequences, learning, and persistence form an ongoing causal loop. The term is functional rather than biological.

## Renderer

A language model or other expression mechanism that converts underlying state into communicable output. In the research program, the renderer should not silently become the owner of identity or canonical state.

## Substrate

The persistent causal medium in which history-dependent organization can be embodied. Examples include recurrent neural dynamics, artificial chemistry, operating-system resources, graph topology, or externally reinjected latent state.

## Phenotype

Externally measurable behavioral structure expressed by an artificial individual under evaluation. It should not be confused with a prompt description or an internal variable named after a personality trait.

## Development

History-dependent change produced by the interaction between a founder architecture and experienced environment over simulated time.

## Developmental attractor

A hypothesis that some structured developmental histories repeatedly move initially similar organisms toward a characteristic mature behavioral region.

## First-person access

Information available to the artificial subject from its own perspective. Raw implementation telemetry can exist without being available to the subject.

## Subject-access firewall

The boundary preventing implementation details, hidden numerical state, objective world truth, evaluator information, or privileged metadata from being silently promoted into first-person knowledge.

## Canonical state

State that is authoritative for what the system believes actually happened within the simulation or organism. Generated prose is not automatically canonical.

## Persistent information asymmetry

A condition in which different actors have lasting differences in what they can perceive, know, process, or infer about the same environment.

## Artificiality symptom

A recurring behavior that makes a persistent character appear mechanically generated, shallowly scripted, or temporally discontinuous despite fluent language.

## Lesion

A controlled destructive intervention used to test where function resides, whether a representation is necessary, and how a system recovers.

## Graft

A controlled transfer of learned structure from one system into another to test sufficiency, localization, and compatibility.

## Same-origin divergence

An experimental design in which identical or tightly matched founders receive different histories and are later evaluated for persistent divergence.

## Matched-history control

A determinism control in which two subjects receive byte-identical histories. Divergence under this condition indicates uncontrolled stochasticity or hidden state.

## Champion

The frozen current best-supported implementation in a comparison framework. It is not modified while a challenger is being evaluated.

## Challenger

A candidate mechanism or architecture tested against the frozen champion under matched scenarios and seeds.
